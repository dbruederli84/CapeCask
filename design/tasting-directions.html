Three ways to put the flavor story at the heart of capecask.com

Today the tasting notes live in one thin strip under a six-row spec table. Each direction below promotes them to a full, signature section — built in the site's own estate-green world, with real motion. Interact with each demo, then tell me which world to build.

CapeCask Redesign Direction Pitch · Sept 2026 All demos live

What we're fixing

From the critique of the current section:

  • The flavor story is a footnote. Four tasting notes — the reason anyone buys a bottle — are compressed into one sentence beside an 84-pixel thumbnail of grapes.
  • Specs outrank the sensory. Distillation facts get six rows of structured attention; what the brandy actually tastes like gets none.
  • No tasting destination. Nothing in the navigation, and no section, answers the first question a brandy drinker asks: what's in the glass?
Direction 01

The Tasting Record

The master distiller's ledger, set in type. Nose, palate and finish read as a horizontal record separated by hairline gold rules, each with a drawn intensity scale. Quiet, editorial, confident — the typographic voice the site already speaks, turned up.

Why it works

  • Strongest fit with the site's editorial voice; feels inevitable, not bolted on.
  • Scales give structure without gimmick — reads at a glance, rewards a slow read.
  • No new assets needed; ships fastest.

Trade-offs

  • Least interactive of the three; motion is entrance-only.
  • Intensity values are claims — you'd need to stand behind them.

Around the notes — in every direction

Whichever world you pick, these supporting improvements ship with it:

  1. Tasting joins the navigation. A "Tasting Notes" anchor between Our Brandy and The Release, so the flavor story is a destination, not a discovery.
  2. The spec list steps back. The six-row technical table stays for the proof-chasers but tightens, and the tasting section takes the room it frees.
  3. Headings carry their own weight. The repeated small-caps eyebrow above every heading goes; the section titles do the talking.
  4. One authored motion per section replaces the identical fade-up on everything — entrances that belong to each section's content, tuned with strong ease-out curves and full reduced-motion fallbacks.
  5. The serving ritual gets a home. "Neat, on the rocks, or in a Brandy Old Fashioned" moves from a caption into the tasting section proper.

Before anything ships

The four notes — rich stone fruit, warm spice, oak & vanilla, long smooth finish — come from your current site.
