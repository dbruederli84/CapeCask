## Platform

web

## Users

US spirits enthusiasts — the barrel-pick, cask-strength, "hazmat bottling" crowd — evaluating an unfamiliar brand pre-release, plus curious premium drinkers who arrive without category knowledge. Their job on this site: decide whether Cape Cask is worth joining the allocation list for, before the first US release exists to buy.

## Product Purpose

Launch site for Cape Cask, a cask-strength South African grape brandy. The site must earn belief in the liquid and convert it into mailing-list signups (allocation access for Batch No. 01) and trade/distribution inquiries. Success = list growth and credible brand presence ahead of US availability.

## Positioning

The only cask-strength South African brandy: pot-distilled in the Cape Winelands, matured in French oak, bottled without dilution at 140+ proof. Cape brandy tradition since 1672 (older than bourbon), with a climate argument (angels' share ~3.5%/yr) analogous to Kentucky's for bourbon.

## Operating Context

Pre-release: not yet available for sale in the United States; distribution inquiries welcomed. Founder-run and deliberately small (founder: Daniel Brüderli, daniel@capecask.com). Signups delivered via StaticForms (public API key by design). Instagram: @capecaskcompany. Single static page deployed at capecask.com.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Static single-page HTML/CSS/JS site, no build step, no framework; images in site/images/.
- Age gate required; session-scoped consent by deliberate choice.
- **NDA constraint: the producing distillery must never be named, credited, linked, or hinted at anywhere on the site, in copy, code comments, commit messages, or metadata.** (Owner-stated 2026-09-01.)
- Three industry reference sites informed inspiration only and must never be named, credited, or linked on the site.
- Tasting descriptors come from the owner-supplied producer fact sheets (received 2026-09-01; the 15-year sibling bottling is the primary source, being nearest to Cape Cask's 16 years). Confirmed vocabulary — nose: ripe/dried fruit, plum, cinnamon, citrus, honey, vanilla, sweet tobacco; palate: oak, dried fruit, coffee, crème brûlée, chocolate, caramel, nuts; finish: elegant, long, velvet-soft. The sheets' accolades belong to the producer's own bottlings and must NEVER be claimed for Cape Cask. Intensity-scale values remain unset (owner to define if ever wanted).
- Owner confirmed (2026-09-01): Batch No. 01, "Cape Gold", 16 years, French oak = ONE bottling, not separate products.
- Copy veto (owner, 2026-09-01): never describe the brandy as "smooth" — to the owner it reads as lack of flavour. Owner-endorsed alternatives, by aspect: velvety (preferred), silky, creamy, supple (texture); polished, mellow, harmonious, balanced (integration); refined, rounded (finish). Avoid "approachable/easy-drinking" — it clashes with the cask-strength positioning.

## Evidence on Hand

Product facts published on the incumbent site (treat as owner-approved): 70%+ ABV / 140+ proof, no dilution; double distillation in copper pot stills at 68–73% ABV, heart cut only; 340-litre French oak, medium char, new and re-used; angels' share ~3.5%/yr; Colombard & Chenin Blanc; master-blended by taste; Cape brandy since 1672. Tasting notes on record: rich stone fruit, warm spice, oak & vanilla, long smooth finish; serve neat, on the rocks, or in a Brandy Old Fashioned.
Photography on hand: cellar, winelands, vineyard, barrels, estate (site/images/). **No bottle or poured-glass photograph exists yet.** No awards, testimonials, or press — do not fabricate any.

## Product Principles

1. Taste is the argument — the flavor story outranks the spec sheet everywhere the two compete.
2. Nothing invented — no tasting claim, award, or fact ships without owner confirmation.
3. Enthusiast-first voice with on-ramps — never dumbed down, never gatekept.
4. Small is a feature — founder-run independence and scarcity are told plainly, not apologized for.
5. The Cape is the differentiator — geography, climate, and heritage carry the premium claim.
